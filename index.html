FLUIDTORIO

Your lander broke up over Strata, a world of stacked, unmixing seas under a waking volcano. Build a floating colony out of materials no one has ever tested. Reach down into the fuel deep and up into the oxidiser sky, and launch before the mountain does.

Nothing here comes with a datasheet. Every chunk you haul in, every joint that creaks, every tower that falls tells you something. The fastest way off this planet is to learn fast.

Hold Shift anywhere to look through a schlieren lens: it shows density itself.

Field manual

The goal

Launch a rocket that reaches escape speed (170 m/s) before the countdown ends. It needs propellant: fuel pumped from the deepest liquid layer, mixed with oxidiser condensed from the highest air layer. Every tonne of rocket has to be pushed, so light, strong tanks matter.

Floating

Every block displaces fluid. Fluid pushes up with the weight of what's displaced (Archimedes). Floats are hollow: light and buoyant, but weak, and they flood if cracked. A heavy, deep keel keeps a tall tower from capsizing. A buoyant keel pushes up and wants to flip you.

Load

Weight flows down through joints to whatever holds it up. Joints carry vertical load well and sideways load poorly. Overloaded joints creak and crack, and break if you don't relieve them: remove load, add a parallel column, or patch them (R). Ductile materials warn you early. Brittle ones barely warn you at all.

Knowing things

Material properties are hidden. The Notebook keeps the range each one could still be in. You learn by:

  • where its chunks float (density bracket), and their waterline (hover a resting chunk)
  • loads its joints have held, and the load where they cracked
  • whether it fizzes in a layer (reaction)
  • Lab tests: exact, but they cost samples and time

The Stress view (V) and build previews use only what you know. Green means certainly fine, amber means it depends on what you don't know yet, and red means certain failure.

Alloys

A Smelter fuses two raw materials. The outcome follows a hidden rule about each material's temper. Some pairs are superb, some are ordinary, some crumble, and one kind of pair explodes. Crucible tests in a Lab are the safe way to find out.
