A game of buoyancy, load paths and experiments

FLUIDTORIO

Your lander broke up over Strata, a world of stacked, unmixing seas under a waking volcano. Build a floating colony out of materials no one has ever tested. Reach down into the fuel deep and up into the oxidiser sky, and launch before the mountain does.

Nothing here comes with a datasheet. Every chunk you haul in, every joint that creaks, every tower that falls tells you something. The fastest way off this planet is to learn fast.

Hold Shift anywhere to look through a schlieren lens: it shows density itself.

Field manual

The goal

Launch a rocket that reaches escape speed (170 m/s) before the volcano erupts. Rockets burn propellant: fuel from the deepest liquid, refined with oxidiser from the highest air.

You start with a raft, a grapple and some salvage. New kinds of block appear as you find a use for them.

Floating

Every block displaces fluid and is pushed up by the weight of what it displaces. Spare lift is how much more you can add before the deck goes under. Floats add lift but are weak. A heavy, deep keel steadies a tall tower; a buoyant keel tries to flip you.

Load

Weight flows down through joints to whatever holds it up. Joints carry vertical load well and sideways load poorly. Overloaded joints creak, crack and snap. Brittle materials barely warn you.

Knowing things

Every material's properties are hidden. The notebook keeps the range each could still be in. You narrow it by:

  • watching where its chunks float, and reading their waterline (hover a resting chunk)
  • seeing which loads its joints hold, and where they crack
  • noticing what fizzes in which layer
  • Lab tests: exact, but they cost samples and time

Previews and the stress view use only what you know. Green is certain, amber depends on what you don't know yet, red will fail.
